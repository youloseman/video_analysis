# The saddle, in millimetres

The report has always known which way your saddle should go — the knee at
the bottom of the stroke is inside its band or it isn't — and has always
said "about 5 mm", because a side view has no length in it.

It has one. Your ankle traces the crank, and a crank is a known length. The
analysis already measures your thigh, your shin and the hip-to-pedal
distance in units of that circle; name the crank and every one of them is
real millimetres. From there the saddle change is one triangle: how much
further (or nearer) the pedal has to sit from the hip for the knee to land
in the middle of its band.

**So the saddle verdict now carries a number: "lower ≈28 mm (±9) to
mid-band, ≈20 mm to reach it".** The fit plan says the same number, and so
does the coach.

Under **Setup & body details** there is a **crank length** field. Without it
172.5 mm is assumed and the tile says so; a 165 mm crank makes the answer 4%
smaller, so it matters less than it looks — but enter yours and the estimate
is yours.

What the ± is for. The knee reading is the largest term: the same clip
mirrored, which cannot change an angle, moves the knee at BDC by up to 3°,
and that goes straight into the millimetres. A clip filmed from the drive
side reads its crank a little small, and the tile says "less certain" on
those. And when the knee is inside the verdict's own tolerance — "acceptable"
— no direction is given: a number telling you to move a saddle the report
just called fine would be the page arguing with itself.

One honest boundary: this is the change *along the line from hip to pedal*,
measured on the leg nearest the camera. Near enough the seat tube for a
first adjustment; ride thirty minutes and film again.
