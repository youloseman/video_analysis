# The helmet, not the ear: a head reading that means something

A reader looked at the Kona figures and asked why every pro scored 100/100
for head position. Fair question. The old score checked whether your ear sat
below a line through your hip and shoulder joints — a line that runs inside
the body — so every rider on aero bars passed, and the helmet, the part that
actually sits in the wind, was never looked at.

**Now Flapp measures the helmet.** From your outline it takes the highest
point of your back, draws a level line through it (level, because the wind
is), and reports how far the top of your helmet rises above it, as a share of
your torso length. The Kona pros sat between +12% and +28%, median +19%;
twenty of the twenty-one were at +23% or lower.

- **Up to +23% costs nothing.** Above that it is amber with a small penalty, never red: the one study we have compares two head positions and draws no line between them.
- **You can move both points.** Where an aero helmet's tail lies flat on your back, the outline cannot always tell back from helmet. Drag the top-of-back and top-of-helmet points into place and the reading follows.
- **It asks you to check when it is unsure** — "check the green point" — and a point you placed by hand clears the flag.
- **Clips and photos agree.** A video reads the helmet on five frames across the clip and takes the median, so the same rider no longer gets a head score on one and a helmet reading on the other.
- **Off the aero bars, or with no helmet on,** the reading is shown but not graded.
