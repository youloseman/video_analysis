# Where you sit over the crank, against the Kona pros

We measured every side-on photo from Kona 2026: 25 pros and 58 age-groupers, with the crank axle and the hip marked on every frame. Saddle height was not what separated them — the knee at the bottom of the stroke came out the same, 145° for the pros and 146° for the age-groupers. Where they sit was.

Draw a line from the crank axle to the hip and measure it from the horizontal behind the rider. The typical pro sat at **88°**, the typical Kona age-grouper at **85°**. Three degrees at the axle is **4–5 cm at the hip**: the pros sit that much further forward. It held for the men and for the women.

Now you can measure your own:

- **After a bike analysis, tap "Mark the crank axle".** Flapp looks for your crank on the chainring and puts the marker there. Check it sits on the centre, drag it if not, then Measure.
- **On a clip, your hip comes from every frame**, not one, so the pelvis rocking through the stroke averages out.
- **You get the angle, where it sits against the pros and the Kona age-groupers, and the gap in centimetres.** Mark the pedal axle for your own scale; with both wheels in the picture, Flapp also levels it on the wheel axles, so a tilted phone isn't read as a position.

It is a field, not a target: it says where the fastest riders sat, not where you must. Moving the saddle forward moves the hip about one for one, but it also changes your reach and the knee over the pedal. One change at a time, 5 mm at a time, then film again.
