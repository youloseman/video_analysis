# Two clips, in step

Before/after compared numbers. Now it compares the clips.

**Compare plays your two overlays side by side, locked stroke to stroke or
step to step.** The earlier clip leads; the later one follows it through
each cycle — the bottom of the pedal stroke on the left lands on the bottom
of the pedal stroke on the right, the foot strike on the left on the foot
strike on the right — whatever the cadence of each. Pause, step a frame,
jump to the next stroke: both move together. It works the same for a left
side against a right side.

This is not a clock. Two clips synchronized by time are in step for one
stride and then never again; two at different frame rates drift apart in
minutes. The lock is on the events the analysis already detected, so the
follower can never be more than one cycle out, and when its cadence differs
it runs a little faster or slower to keep pace — the readout says by how
much. When one clip has fewer cycles it repeats them.

When the detected cycles of the two clips don't agree — a far-away clip
where half the foot strikes were missed, say — the panel says so and plays
the two from their own starts instead of pretending. And, as before: two
clips are two camera setups. Compare the positions, not the pixels.
