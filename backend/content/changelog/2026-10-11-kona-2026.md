# Twenty-one Kona pros, and the rules that moved

After the Ironman World Championship we ran professional bike positions from
race photographs through the same pose model Flapp uses on your clips, checked
every joint point by hand, and wrote up what the field actually rides:
[Kona 2026: What 21 Pro Bike Positions Actually Measure](/academy/kona-2026-pro-bike-positions).
The median pro rode with the forearms tilted 25° up, the torso at 21°, the
shoulder at 87° and the helmet 19% of a torso length above the back.

Measuring the world's best against our own ranges showed some of our
aero-bar rules were behind them. Each moved only as far as a source supports:

- **Forearm tilt runs to 30°.** Every pro was at 19° or more, and half sat above 25°, where our range used to end.
- **A closed shoulder on aero bars is amber, never red,** and costs a third of the penalty. No fit source calls it a fault; one opened past the range still costs full rate.
- **A torso flatter than the range costs no points** on aero bars. It comes with one plain caveat instead: faster through the air, but most age-groupers cannot hold it and make their power.
- **The aero read-out splits the low positions** into three steps on measured frontal areas, instead of putting every torso under 28° in one bucket.

The photo analysis got sharper on the way. A bike photo now says where the
crank was — bottom, top, or "not sure" — and grades the knee and hip only at
a position it can name, so a saddle that is too low can be seen from a still.
The knee in a photo is graded on the same slope as on a clip, so a photo and
a clip of one saddle cannot disagree about how far off it is. And a very
large photo that found no body is retried at a smaller size before you are
told there is nobody in it.
