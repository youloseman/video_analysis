# Angles, in the convention you read

Every joint angle here is measured as the angle *between* two segments: a
straight knee reads 180°. That is how a goniometer on the frame reads it,
and it is not how most people write it down. A physio writes knee *flexion*
— 0° straight, 90° bent. A bike-fit table says "35–45° of knee extension at
the bottom" and means 135–145° the way we measure it. The same joint, the
same position, two numbers.

**There is now a switch: Included angle or Anatomical.** It sits over the
joint-angle table and over the player's curve, and it changes every printed
angle on the page — the tiles, the table, the curve, the chips on the
skeleton layer — and the reference bands with them, so "35–42° flexion" is
the same band as "138–145° included".

What it does not change is a verdict. Every grade is computed on the
measured value before the number is written, and flipping the convention
cannot move a reading in or out of its band. The annotated render keeps the
included angle; the ankle on a bike (knee–ankle–toe) is not a clinical joint
angle and stays as measured.

Also on the player, on the **Skeleton layer**: a **trail** — the path one
joint has just travelled, fading into the past. The foot, knee or hip on a
run; the ankle, knee or hip on a bike. A pedal circle that isn't a circle,
a foot that lands ahead of the hip: things a number states and a trail
shows. It is withheld on a clip whose camera was moving, because then the
path in the picture is the camera's, not the joint's.
