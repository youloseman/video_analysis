# The video has a player now

The annotated overlay was a picture of the analysis: skeleton, chips, a phase
bar, all burned into an mp4 with the browser's own controls under it. You
could watch it. You could not ask it anything — where the knee is at 2.3 s,
which frame the foot lands on, what the hip does across a stroke.

**The overlay now plays inside a player that knows what it is showing.**
Under the video:

- **Frame stepping** (◀ ▶, or the arrow keys) and **¼× / ½× speed**, so a
  moment is found rather than paused at.
- **A strip you can drag** — on a run it shows every stance and swing; on a
  bike, every other revolution is shaded, BDC to BDC.
- **Jump to the next event**: the next foot strike or toe-off, the next BDC or
  TDC. These are the events the analysis detected — nobody has to mark them.
- **The angle curve.** One joint at a time, over the whole clip, with the
  reference band shaded and a playhead that moves with the video. Click the
  curve to jump there. It is the same filtered series the table reads, so the
  number on the joint and the shape under the video cannot disagree.

Where a band belongs to one moment rather than the whole cycle — the bike knee
at the bottom of the stroke, the hip at the top — the curve stays plain and
the dots at those moments carry the colour. A knee at 90° mid-stroke is not
out of range; it is mid-stroke.

While your clip is still in the browser, two more **layers**: the bare
skeleton over your own footage, and the footage alone. The overlay render is
what stays on the server; the original is yours, and the layers only exist
for as long as you have it open.

Saved reports get the same player over their stored overlay — everything above
except the layers.
