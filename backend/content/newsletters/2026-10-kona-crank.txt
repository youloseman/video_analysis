Kona pros don't sit higher. They sit further forward.

Hi,

We spent last week measuring every side-on photo from Kona 2026: 25 pros and 58 age-groupers on the Queen K. On every frame we marked the hip and the crank axle, then compared the two groups.

WHAT WE FOUND

It isn't saddle height. The knee at the bottom of the stroke came out the same for both groups: 145° for the pros, 146° for the age-groupers.

It's where you sit. Draw a line from the crank axle to the hip and measure it from the horizontal behind the rider. The typical pro sat at 88° (middle half 87–90°), the typical Kona age-grouper at 85° (83–87°). Three degrees at the axle is 4–5 cm at the hip: that's how much further forward the pros sit.

It held for the men and for the women. The pros were also a couple of degrees flatter in the torso, without closing the angle at the hip: sitting further forward is what lets them get low.

Two honest limits. These age-groupers all qualified for Kona, so the gap to a typical amateur is probably bigger. And this describes where the fastest riders sat; it isn't a rule for your bike.
