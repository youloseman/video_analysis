In September 2026 the UCI published its first position statement on nutrition for cycling: 54 researchers, 14 reviews, every discipline from BMX to Grand Tours. Here is the short version, and the part of it that shows up on your bike.

## Five points worth keeping

1. **Fuel for the race you are doing.** Under about 75 minutes, a carbohydrate mouth rinse is enough. From 1 to 2.5 hours, about 30 to 60 g an hour. Longer than that, around 90 g an hour, from drinks and gels that mix glucose and fructose, which the gut absorbs faster together. Many pros now take 90 to 120 g and more; the statement treats that as practice, not a proven target.
2. **Train your gut.** Large intakes are tolerated better when they are practiced in training, in the forms you will race with.
3. **Lighter is not always faster.** Chasing body mass with too little energy can harm hormones, bones and performance (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport, REDs). Get expert advice before you cut weight.
4. **Few supplements work.** Caffeine, creatine, nitrate, sodium bicarbonate and beta-alanine have evidence for specific uses; most of what is sold does not. Use batch-tested products.
5. **Plan the day, not only the ride.** Fuel for the work you are doing that day; for an event where it matters, the statement's authors describe 1 to 2 days of carbohydrate loading.

## Where your position comes in

Ninety grams an hour is a typical 30 g gel every 20 minutes, plus your bottles. Every one of those is a hand off the bars, and on a TT or triathlon bike it often means sitting up.

- **The position that matters is the one you can eat in.** The seconds you lose sitting up for a gel are small. The minutes you lose when you stop eating late in a race are not.
- **Practice reaching your food, not only digesting it.** Put bottles and gels where you can get them without leaving the extensions for long, and rehearse it on long rides.
- **Film at race effort.** Joint angles change with effort: in one 3D study the elbow bent more as intensity rose. A clip at an easy spin shows a position you don't race in. Film 10 to 15 seconds at race effort, after a couple of hours if you can, and check what holds.

## What Flapp measures, and what it doesn't

Flapp doesn't measure what you eat. It reads your position from a side-on clip: trunk, hip, knee, elbow and shoulder, and on aero bars a relative drag zone. Put the two together and you can see whether the position you race in is one you can hold, and fuel in, for the whole race.
