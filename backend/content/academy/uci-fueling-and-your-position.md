In September 2026 the UCI published its first position statement on nutrition for cycling: 54 researchers, 14 reviews, every discipline from BMX to Grand Tours. Here is the short version, then the part a triathlete feels on the bike: how often you leave the bars to eat, what that costs, and how to check your own feeding position.

## Five points worth keeping

1. **Fuel for the race you are doing.** Under about 75 minutes, a carbohydrate mouth rinse is enough. From 1 to 2.5 hours, about 30 to 60 g an hour. Longer, around 90 g an hour, from drinks and gels that mix glucose and fructose. Many pros take 90 to 120 g and more; the statement treats that as practice, not a proven target.
2. **Train your gut.** Large intakes are tolerated better when practiced in training, in the forms you will race with.
3. **Lighter is not always faster.** Too little energy harms hormones, bones and performance (REDs). Get expert advice before you cut weight.
4. **Few supplements work.** Caffeine, creatine, nitrate, sodium bicarbonate and beta-alanine have evidence for specific uses. Use batch-tested products.
5. **Plan the day, not only the ride.** For a race that matters, the authors describe 1 to 2 days of carbohydrate loading.

## How often you leave the bars

Take those targets to a triathlon bike leg: a 30 g gel for each 30 g of carbohydrate, and a drink every 15 minutes.

![How often you leave the bars to eat and drink: 7 times in an Olympic bike leg, 19 in a 70.3, 40 in an Ironman.](/media/academy/uci-fueling/feeds.webp)

| Race | Gels + drinks | Out of aero |
|---|---|---|
| Olympic, 40 km | 2 + 5 = 7 | ~1.5 min |
| 70.3, 90 km | 8 + 11 = 19 | ~4 min |
| Ironman, 180 km | 17 + 23 = 40 | ~8 min |

Forty times in an Ironman. If each one means sitting up, that is eight minutes of the bike leg spent out of your race position.

## What it costs

We rode each bike leg through Flapp's aero model at the same power three times: all of it in the race position, with every feed taken on the base bar, and sitting up for every feed.

![What eating and drinking cost you: Olympic +3 s on the base bar or +6 s sitting up, 70.3 +8 s or +17 s, Ironman +16 s or +35 s.](/media/academy/uci-fueling/cost.webp)

| Race | All in aero | Feeds on the base bar | Feeds sitting up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Olympic | 1:08:34 | +3 s | +6 s |
| 70.3 | 2:43:38 | +8 s | +17 s |
| Ironman | 5:48:23 | +16 s | +35 s |

Sitting up for all 40 feeds in an Ironman costs about half a minute, less than 0.2% of the bike split. Running out of fuel in the last hour costs far more. So eat, even if it means sitting up.

A bottle between the arms takes most of this away: 23 of the 40 Ironman feeds are drinks, and you take them without leaving the extensions.

## Check yours with Flapp: three photos

Flapp reads your torso angle from a side photo. Take three, on the trainer, at race effort:

1. **Race position.** On the extensions, as you ride for hours.
2. **Reaching for a bottle.** One hand on the base bar, the other on the bottle.
3. **Sitting up to eat.** A gel in hand, as you take it in a race.

Upload each as a bike photo, note the trunk angle, and read it on the scale below.

![Your torso angle, read as drag: race position 20° at 78% of upright drag, reaching for a bottle 33° at 87%, sitting up to eat 45° at 100%.](/media/academy/uci-fueling/scale.webp)

What to look for:

- **Photo 1 tells you your race number.** The Kona 2026 pros sat at a median of 20°, measured with Flapp. Most age-groupers are higher, and that is fine if they hold it.
- **The gap between 1 and 2 is your bottle setup.** If reaching for a bottle takes you 10° or more up, move the bottle where you can get it lower.
- **Photo 3 shows how far up you go to eat.** If it is close to upright, take gels from a bento box or a flask on the extensions, and practice it on long rides.

Then film a 10 to 15 second clip in the race position, at race effort, after a couple of hours if you can. Joint angles change with effort: in one 3D study the elbow bent more as intensity rose. A clip at an easy spin shows a position you don't race in, and the position you hold for hours is worth more than the one you hit for a minute.

## How these numbers were made

The feed counts use the UCI targets above, 30 g gels, a drink every 15 minutes and about 12 seconds per feed. Bike speeds are 35, 33 and 31 km/h. The drag scale is Flapp's aero model: relative drag by torso angle, interpolated between postures measured in the studies below, and an estimate, not a CdA. Above 33° the model has postures, not measured angles, which is why that part of the line is dashed. The race position is the Kona pros' median; the two feeding angles are typical values, and your three photos replace them with your own.

Flapp doesn't measure what you eat. It measures the position you eat in.
