    How to film it

    Three things decide whether the numbers are worth reading. Don’t overthink the rest — a phone propped against a water bottle works.

    1. Fill the frame. Head to feet should span about two thirds of it. Sky above and ground below are what wreck the measurement — this rule is worth more than every other one here combined.

    2. Hold the phone upright. Portrait puts nearly twice as many pixels on your legs as landscape.

    3. Film from the side, camera at hip height. A few degrees off exactly 90° actually helps separate the legs. Turning to follow you is fine — it’s up-and-down shake that hurts, not panning.

    A few more things that help
    • Stand back 6–8 m and zoom in 2–3× rather than filming close — a close-up wide lens bends the angles at the edges
    • On the bike, film from the non-drive side (chainring away from the camera) — the chainring sits right behind your ankle at the bottom of the stroke and can pull the tracking off your foot
    • 5–10 seconds with you actually in shot, one person in frame — start recording once you’re already running, not before
    • A treadmill is the easiest way to get all of this at once: you stay put, so the camera can sit close, still, and pointed at one spot
    • Normal speed or slow-mo — both work. Slow-mo sharpens joint detail; its cadence & timing are estimates and aren’t graded
    • Light behind the camera, not behind you — a silhouette loses limbs
